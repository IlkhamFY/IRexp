\abstract{%
Machine-learning models for infrared (IR) spectroscopy are often trained on computed
spectra, yet simulated spectra differ from measured ones, and large, open experimental
IR datasets remain scarce.
Here, we introduce IRexp, an open collection of 230,946 experimental IR band lists
(cm$^{-1}$ peak positions) extracted from 19,859 open-access articles published between
2005 and 2026 and their supporting information, together with an author-entered
Chemotion repository deposit.
In total, 96,973 records are linked to a resolved structure, and 50,682 of these also
carry author-reported $^{1}$H and $^{13}$C NMR.
Record quality was checked manually against the source documents and by automated
consistency tests.
IRexp holds more than four times as many experimental IR records as existing public IR
spectral libraries, and every record keeps its source identifier and licence, with records released in
licence-specific files.
We expect IRexp to accelerate data-driven IR spectral prediction, automated
structure elucidation, and multimodal models trained on real experimental data.
}

\section{Background \& Summary}\label{sec:background}

Infrared (IR) spectroscopy is routine in the characterisation of synthetic organic
compounds~\citep{silverstein2014sioc,blum2012atrftir}.
Experimental sections and supporting information often report an IR band list
(wavenumbers in cm$^{-1}$) for a compound, alongside $^{1}$H and $^{13}$C NMR shift lists.
In recent years, machine-learning models have begun to predict structures from IR
spectra~\citep{alberts2024irstruct,chacko2024spectro,sondhi2025jirvis}.
However, the largest open spectral datasets used to train such models are
computed~\citep{zipoli2025uspto,alberts2024multimodal,krishnadas2026squirl}.
Simulated spectra differ from measured ones, so models trained on them may perform
worse on real measurements; the same argument has been made for NMR~\citep{wang2025nmrexp}.
Better models therefore need large amounts of real experimental data.
Moreover, the text band list itself is what several structure-elucidation tools take
as input: computer-assisted structure elucidation (CASE) systems combine IR
characteristic-frequency tables with NMR~\citep{elyashberg2009case}, and
database-independent elucidators accept author-style IR, $^{1}$H/$^{13}$C NMR, and MS
lists~\citep{pesek2021elucidation}.
Yet open, redistributable collections of experimental IR data remain sparse for machine
learning and for findable, accessible, interoperable, and reusable (FAIR)
reuse~\citep{wilkinson2016fair}.

To date, public experimental IR resources are smaller and view-only.
The NIST Chemistry WebBook~\citep{nist_webbook} and AIST SDBS~\citep{sdbs} provide
digitised experimental spectra rather than text band lists.
In contrast, the largest open IR datasets are computed: Zipoli \textit{et al.} released
an IR--NMR multimodal dataset of computed spectra~\citep{zipoli2025uspto}, Alberts
\textit{et al.} released a computational multimodal spectroscopic dataset that includes
IR~\citep{alberts2024multimodal}, and Krishnadas \textit{et al.} built a
quantum-chemistry infrared resonance library~\citep{krishnadas2026squirl}.
IR-to-structure and spectral-embedding models, including Spectro,
j-IR-vis~\citep{chacko2024spectro,sondhi2025jirvis}, and transformer elucidation from
IR spectra~\citep{alberts2024irstruct,alberts2024multimodal}, operate on digitised
absorbance traces rather than literature band lists.
For other spectroscopies, MassBank shares experimental mass-spectral peaks with
persistent identifiers~\citep{horai2010massbank,neumann2026massbank}, NMRexp provides
a literature-mined NMR peak-list corpus~\citep{wang2025nmrexp}, and NMRTrans reports
experimental NMR spectra and the NMRSpec corpus~\citep{yang2026nmrtrans}.
To address this gap for IR, we collect literature-reported experimental IR band lists
together with source identifiers and licence information.

Here, we introduce IRexp, a collection of 230,946 experimental IR band lists mined from
PMC Open Access full text and supporting information and from a Chemotion repository
deposit~\citep{tremouilhac2017chemotion,tremouilhac2020chemotionrepo,chemotion2024}.
The pipeline harvests open-access text from the PMC Open Access Subset S3 mirror and
author-entered band lists from the Chemotion FT-IR deposit.
It extracts numeric band lists and any co-reported NMR strings, and it resolves compound
names to structures with OPSIN~\citep{lowe2011opsin} and RDKit~\citep{landrum_rdkit},
recording InChIKey~\citep{heller2013inchi} and SELFIES~\citep{krenn2020selfies} when
resolution succeeds.
In total, 96,973 records are structure-linked, and 50,682 of these also carry
author-reported $^{1}$H and $^{13}$C NMR.
Figure~\ref{fig:positioning} shows one record from printed text to band list (a),
IRexp beside other open IR resources (b), and the chemical space of its
structure-linked molecules (c, d).

Beyond its scale, IRexp is built for traceable reuse.
Each record carries a source identifier and a licence-pool
stamp~\citep{hrynaszkiewicz2012open,carroll2011openaccess}.
Records hold extracted band positions, identifiers, and short author-text fields
copied from the source (NMR shift strings and, on supporting-information rows, the
printed IR string and compound name); PDFs, figures, and article full text are not
redistributed.
The CC-BY file is the dataset of record, and the non-commercial and ShareAlike pools
are separate files (Section~\ref{sec:datarecords}).
IRexp does not replace absorbance-curve libraries when full spectra are needed, or
computed collections when simulated coverage is needed~\citep{zipoli2025uspto}.
Overall, we expect IRexp to support multimodal pretraining, supervised IR-to-structure
modelling on structure-linked rows, and literature-based benchmarks for structure
elucidation.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Source corpora}

\paragraph{PMC Open Access Subset.}
The main harvest uses the NCBI PMC Open Access bulk distribution on Amazon S3
(\texttt{s3://pmc-oa-opendata}, HTTPS endpoint
\texttt{https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com})~\citep{pmc_oa}.
We found identifiers with NCBI E-utilities \texttt{esearch} over PMC.
The search ran month by month, newest first, over publication years 2005--2026 (up to
9,000 identifiers per month), with the term
\texttt{IR\_MARK AND <year>/<month>[pdat] AND open access[filter]}, where
\texttt{IR\_MARK} is
\texttt{("IR (KBr" OR "IR (neat" OR "IR (ATR" OR "IR (film" OR "IR (CHCl" OR
"FT-IR (" OR "FTIR (" OR "IR (thin" OR "$\nu$max" OR "$\nu$ max")}.
We then fetched the matching plain-text objects from the flat S3 keys
\texttt{PMC<id>.<v>/PMC<id>.<v>.txt}.
The crawl ran on 2026-06-07 (UTC).
Its list of the 220,159 scanned PMC identifiers (\texttt{seen\_papers.txt.gz}) is part
of the deposit, so the snapshot can be rebuilt without a new query.
In addition, supporting-information (SI) files were read for 37,724 of these papers.
In total, the deposit holds records from 19,859 PMC accessions.

\paragraph{Licences.}
PMC OA content is not under a single licence~\citep{pmc_oa}, so each record takes the
licence of its source article.
Specifically, main-text rows were joined to Europe PMC~\citep{rosonovski2024europepmc}
\texttt{license} metadata (15,416 PMCIDs; 2026-08-26/27), and SI rows take the licence
from the PMC OA S3 metadata.
Rows still without a licence were then checked against explicit Creative Commons links in
Crossref~\citep{kemp2022crossref}, a fresh Europe PMC query, and the PMC OA S3 metadata
and article XML; publisher text-and-data-mining links were not used.
The 8,789 rows without an established Creative Commons licence are not included.
In total, 230,946 of 239,735 harvested records are deposited (Figure~\ref{fig:distribution}a,
Table~\ref{tab:files}).

\paragraph{Chemotion / RADAR4Chem.}
1,888 records come from the Chemotion Repository~\citep{tremouilhac2020chemotionrepo}
FT-IR collection (ELN ecosystem~\citep{tremouilhac2017chemotion}), deposited at
RADAR4Chem (DOI \texttt{10.22000/OGoEQGlsZGElrgst}) under
CC-BY-SA-4.0~\citep{chemotion2024} and ingested on 2026-06-07.
We downloaded the MD5-verified deposit, flattened the Quill-delta \texttt{content}
field of each of the 2,116 ATR-IR analyses to plain text, and parsed the
author-curated band list with the same extractor and construction filters as the
PMC path.
Deposit SMILES were canonicalised with RDKit and encoded as InChIKey and SELFIES.
We kept the richest band list per InChIKey and dropped 8 molecules already in the
PMC pool. This gave 1,888 new rows.
All of them carry a deposit structure: 1,802 are released as structure-linked
(\texttt{has\_structure=true}; tier~D, because the rows have no NMR), and 86 salt or
invalid structures are withheld (tier~X).
On the 1,802 structure-linked rows \texttt{id} equals \texttt{inchikey}, the 86
withheld rows carry opaque ids (Section~\ref{sec:objdef}), \texttt{license} is
\texttt{CC-BY-SA-4.0}, \texttt{source} is \texttt{Chemotion}, and
\texttt{source\_doi} is the deposit DOI.
Authors enter these band lists in the electronic laboratory notebook; they are not
peak-picked from absorbance curves.
As a result, they are denser than paper prose (median 39 bands against 10 for PMC).

\paragraph{Excluded sources.}
No records were taken from AIST SDBS~\citep{sdbs} or the NIST Chemistry
WebBook~\citep{nist_webbook}.

\subsection{Extraction}

A deterministic regular-expression pipeline (\texttt{code/spectro\_scraper/extract.py})
splits experimental text by compound.
It then writes IR wavenumbers to \texttt{ir\_bands\_cm-1} (a list of floats in cm$^{-1}$)
and any $^{1}$H and $^{13}$C author strings to \texttt{h\_nmr} and \texttt{c\_nmr},
complementing general chemistry text-mining toolkits such as
ChemDataExtractor~\citep{swain2016chemdataextractor}.
Simple gates reject scan-range artefacts and common prose false positives.
In addition, the parser strips US thousands separators before matching, so a grouping
comma does not split a wavenumber (PMC6268696 reports $2{,}976$\,cm$^{-1}$, which would
otherwise be read as 976).
Duplicate integer wavenumbers within a list are removed.
Figures are not digitised, and JCAMP-DX is not stored.

\paragraph{IR attachment.}
The extractor assigns each IR block within the compound's own section of the
experimental text, bounded by compound headings and paragraph breaks.
It does not attach the nearest IR block to an NMR record.
A record without its own IR block gets no IR, and IR-only compounds become their own
records.
The main-text harvest gave 116,320 PMC rows, of which 107,531 are deposited.

\paragraph{Supporting information.}
The same parser read the SI files of PMC OA papers.
It added 121,527 band lists from 5,688 papers, all of which are deposited (78,053 CC-BY/CC0, 15,566 CC-BY-NC-ND, and
27,908 non-commercial).
SI rows pass the same construction filters as main-text rows (at least three bands,
400--4000\,cm$^{-1}$, and within-list integer deduplication).
Moreover, 1,836 rows already present in the main-text corpus were removed.
On SI rows \texttt{ir\_source} is \texttt{experimental\_si}, the author IR string is
kept in \texttt{ir\_raw}, and the compound label and name are kept in
\texttt{si\_label} and \texttt{si\_name}.

\paragraph{Quality gates.}
At least three bands and wavenumbers within 400--4000\,cm$^{-1}$ are construction
filters (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}).
All 2,771,767 deposited band values lie inside the window.
Formula--NMR checks in \texttt{spectro\_scraper.quality} ($^{1}$H integral sum
$\leq$ formula hydrogen count $+2$; $^{13}$C peak count $\leq$ carbon count) are
diagnostic flags applied after construction.
Flagged rows stay in the release files, and Technical Validation reports the
quarantine on all structure-linked rows.

\subsection{Structure resolution}

To link records to structures, compound names in the record's own block are converted with
OPSIN~\citep{lowe2011opsin}, canonicalised with RDKit~\citep{landrum_rdkit}, and
encoded as InChIKey~\citep{heller2013inchi} and SELFIES~\citep{krenn2020selfies}.
Three other routes are used.
These are a label-to-name map within the same paper, a PubChem~\citep{kim2023pubchem}
name lookup through the PUG REST service (name to isomeric SMILES), used mainly as the
independent second method for tier~A, and optical chemical structure recognition (OCSR)
of compound drawings in the SI with MolScribe~\citep{qian2023molscribe}.
Trade names, mixtures, and non-IUPAC prose often fail; unresolved rows keep
\texttt{has\_structure=false} and null structure fields.

Every released structure carries an evidence tier, \texttt{structure\_tier} (Table~\ref{tab:tiers}).
Tier~A requires a formula or HRMS match in the compound's own block and an
independent second method that agrees on the InChIKey-14 (PubChem on the name, or
OCSR on the drawing).
OPSIN on the same name string does not count as independent, and OCSR alone never
reaches tier~A.
Tier~X rows keep their band lists, but their structure is withheld
(\texttt{has\_structure=false}).
Formula mismatches that look like typing errors in the source are flagged
(\texttt{formula\_conflict}) rather than excluded.

In the deposit, 2,132 structure-linked rows name PubChem in their method fields, 2,111
of them in tier~A.

\paragraph{Chemical-space map.}
Figures~\ref{fig:positioning}c and \ref{fig:distribution}c place the 92,777 unique
structure-linked molecules (InChIKey) on a UMAP~\citep{mcinnes2018umap} embedding of
Morgan fingerprints~\citep{rogers2010ecfp} (radius 2,
2,048 bits; truncated SVD to 50 components; cosine metric, 30 neighbours, minimum
distance 0.1, seed 42).
Islands are HDBSCAN~\citep{campello2013hdbscan} clusters on the map
(scikit-learn~\citep{pedregosa2011sklearn}) (minimum cluster size 600, minimum samples 20,
leaf selection).
An island is named by its most enriched substructure (SMARTS) when that substructure
occurs in at least 50\% of its members and at least three times as often as in all
molecules; other islands are left unnamed.
A molecule found in several licence pools takes the most open one
(CC-BY/CC0, then ShareAlike, then non-commercial, then no-derivatives source).

\section{Data Records}\label{sec:datarecords}

Each IRexp record is the experimental IR band list of one compound from one source.
It is stored as a JSON object with its source identifier and licence stamp and, where
available, co-reported $^{1}$H and $^{13}$C NMR strings and a resolved structure
(Table~\ref{tab:fields}).
In total, the deposit holds 230,946 records: 229,058 from PMC (107,531 main text and
121,527 SI, from 19,859 PMC accessions) and 1,888 from Chemotion
(Figure~\ref{fig:distribution}a).
Of these, 116,366 (50.4\%) carry $^{1}$H or $^{13}$C NMR strings, 96,973 are
structure-linked, 59,783 have a structure and at least one NMR string, and 50,682 carry
IR, $^{1}$H NMR, $^{13}$C NMR, and a structure.
The records hold 2,771,767 band values; median band counts are 9 (PMC main text), 11
(PMC SI), 10 (PMC overall), and 39 (Chemotion) (Figure~\ref{fig:distribution}b).
The dataset of record is the CC-BY file (\texttt{irexp\_commercial.jsonl.gz}; 190,049
records; CC-BY-4.0)~\citep{hrynaszkiewicz2012open}.
It contains extracted experimental fields only: band positions, short author report
strings, compound labels and names, identifiers, and resolved structures.
No article text, PDFs, or figures are included.
Specifically, it holds the 164,016 CC-BY/CC0 records and the 26,033 records from articles under
no-derivatives licences (26,028 CC-BY-NC-ND and 5 CC-BY-ND), which are flagged
\texttt{nd\_source\_licence=true}.
Non-commercial (39,000; CC-BY-NC and CC-BY-NC-SA) and ShareAlike (1,897) records are in
separate files under their source licences (Table~\ref{tab:files}).
Every record keeps its source licence (\texttt{license}, \texttt{license\_raw},
\texttt{license\_pool}) and its source DOI or PMCID.
Users who need source licences that permit adaptation can drop the rows with
\texttt{nd\_source\_licence=true}.
For structure or NMR training, the ids in \texttt{structure\_quarantine.jsonl.gz} should be
dropped, tiers A--B used where a formula-verified structure is needed, and tier-D
structures treated as candidates.

\subsection{Object definition}\label{sec:objdef}

The \texttt{id} field is a stable key, not a key for joining molecules.
Most rows (204,144) carry a 20-character hexadecimal key assigned at harvest from the
extracted content.
On 23,795 rows with a released structure, including the
Chemotion rows, \texttt{id} equals \texttt{inchikey}.
The 3,007 withheld tier-X rows whose harvest id was InChIKey-shaped carry an opaque
key (\texttt{wx} followed by 20 hexadecimal characters), so that the public id does
not reveal the withheld structure.
The InChIKey is stored in \texttt{inchikey} and is not unique: the 96,973
structure-linked records contain 92,777 InChIKeys.
\texttt{source\_doi} is a source identifier and is not always a DOI.
In addition, the CC-BY file has quality-control (QC) columns (\texttt{ir\_bands\_old\_cm-1},
\texttt{ir\_shared\_in\_paper}, \texttt{ir\_table\_flatten\_suspect},
\texttt{ir\_refetch\_confirmed}, and \texttt{ir\_refetch\_merged}); the boolean
columns are flags only, and no rows were dropped for them.
They are present on the CC-BY/CC0 rows and on SI rows; most other main-text rows carry
\texttt{ir\_shared\_in\_paper} only.
On SI rows \texttt{ir\_table\_flatten\_suspect} is null because it was not computed.
Listing~\ref{lst:example} shows an excerpt of a structure-linked CC-BY record
with nine IR bands from PMC13029360, a resolved structure, and co-reported $^{1}$H
and $^{13}$C NMR strings.

\section{Technical Validation}\label{sec:techval}

\subsection{Human audit against the source}

To evaluate accuracy, R.S. checked two stratified samples against their source
documents (Figure~\ref{fig:distribution}d).
In 161 main-text records, the source was found for 160, bands were exact for 143 and an
acceptable subset for 13, lists were complete for 149, and name and structure agreed for
132 of the 133 structure-linked records judged.
The most common errors were mass-spectrometry or other non-IR numbers captured in IR
lists.
In 150 SI records (seed 20261018; 30 tier-A, 40 tier-B, 15 tier-C, 15 tier-D, and 50
no-structure records), band lists were complete and correct for 145, and structures
were correct for 90 of 100: 68 of 70 in tiers A--B, 14 of 15 in tier~C, and 8 of 15 in
tier~D (Table~\ref{tab:audit150}).
Moreover, the list belonged to the entry named by the record's label and name, with
that label and name correct (compound entry), for 85 of 100 structure-linked and 13 of
50 no-structure records.

\subsection{Automated checks}

We also ran automated checks on a stratified sample of 201 records (seed 20261110).
In total, 2,131 of 2,133 bands were found in the source text of the 195 records with
cached text; 6 SI records had none and are not scored.
In addition, rerunning the extractor on the source text of 120 paper--source units recovered 1,420 of
1,589 released lists.
These checks show that band values occur in the source, not that a list belongs to the
assigned compound.
The formula--NMR checks ($^{13}$C peak count $\leq$ carbon count; $^{1}$H integral sum
$\leq$ formula H$+2$) flag 299 of the 96,973 structure-linked records (0.31\%).
Flagged rows stay in the files, and their ids are listed with the 8,158 withheld
tier-X ids in \texttt{data/structure\_quarantine.jsonl.gz}.
Figure~\ref{fig:distribution}a--c shows the composition, band counts, and chemical
coverage of the deposit.
