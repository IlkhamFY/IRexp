\abstract{%
IRexp is a collection of experimental infrared band lists
(cm$^{-1}$ peak positions) mined from open chemistry literature, optionally with
author-reported $^{1}$H/$^{13}$C NMR strings and resolved structures.
The multi-licence research corpus holds 239,735 records (237,847 PMC Open Access
text and supplements; 1,888 Chemotion/RADAR4Chem), including 101,624
structure-linked entries and 54,429
IR~$+$~$^{1}$H~$+$~$^{13}$C~$+$~structure quadruples.
Records are numeric band lists, not absorbance traces, each with a source
identifier and a stamped licence.
The public dataset is the commercial CC-BY/CC0 pool of 163,931 records,
packaged under CC-BY-4.0.
ShareAlike and non-commercial records are separate Hub companions under their
source licences and are excluded from that pool.
Intended reuse is multimodal training, retrieval, and tool input.
Technical validation covers automated transcription, recall proxies, and
consistency audits before and after separator repair, quarantine, and an expert
human band-list audit of 161 records (not rescored).
Complementary elucidation benchmarks are described in a companion manuscript
and are not analysed here.
}

\section{Background \& Summary}\label{sec:background}

IRexp is a collection of experimental IR band lists
(cm$^{-1}$ positions) mined from PMC Open Access full text and from a
Chemotion electronic laboratory notebook / repository
deposit~\citep{tremouilhac2017chemotion,tremouilhac2020chemotionrepo,chemotion2024}.
Each record carries a source identifier and a stamped licence
pool~\citep{hrynaszkiewicz2012open,carroll2011openaccess}.
Corpus size and the public commercial file are in
Section~\ref{sec:datarecords}.

Infrared (IR) spectroscopy is routine in materials characterisation~\citep{silverstein2014sioc,blum2012atrftir}, yet open,
redistributable collections of \emph{experimental} IR data remain sparse relative
to modern machine-learning needs and FAIR reuse goals.\footnote{FAIR: Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable~\citep{wilkinson2016fair}.}
Digitised absorbance libraries such as the NIST Chemistry WebBook~\citep{nist_webbook}
and AIST SDBS~\citep{sdbs} are valuable but either modest in size or view-only
(no bulk redistribution).
Adjacent community resources show what a reusable spectral object can look like:
MassBank exposes experimental mass-spectral peaks with persistent
identifiers~\citep{horai2010massbank,neumann2026massbank}; NMRexp provides a
literature-mined NMR peak-list corpus at Data Descriptor
scale~\citep{wang2025nmrexp}; and computational infrared sets in
\textit{Scientific Data} supply simulated spectra, including an IR--NMR
multimodal collection and an infrared resonance
library~\citep{zipoli2025uspto,krishnadas2026squirl}.
A computational multimodal spectroscopic dataset that includes infrared was
released in the NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks
track~\citep{alberts2024multimodal}.
Literature-mined numeric tables in chemistry more broadly, for example
BigSolDB~\citep{krasnov2025bigsoldb}, likewise illustrate the Data Descriptor
format.
NMRTrans reports experimental NMR spectra and the NMRSpec
corpus~\citep{yang2026nmrtrans}.
IRexp supplies the corresponding experimental object for infrared band lists.
It is not a replacement for absorbance-curve libraries when full digitised
spectra are required, nor a simulated multimodal corpus when computed coverage
is required~\citep{zipoli2025uspto}.

Experimental sections of chemistry papers conventionally report per-compound
IR band lists (wavenumbers in cm$^{-1}$) together with $^{1}$H/$^{13}$C NMR shift
lists.
That textual convention is the object many elucidation pipelines consume, and it
differs from a digitised spectrum.
Computer-assisted structure elucidation (CASE) systems ingest IR
characteristic-frequency peak tables together with NMR~\citep{elyashberg2009case},
and database-independent elucidators take author-style IR, $^{1}$H/$^{13}$C NMR,
and MS tabular lists as input~\citep{pesek2021elucidation}.
Related IR-to-structure and spectral-embedding models that operate on digitised
absorbance traces rather than literature band lists include Spectro and
j-IR-vis~\citep{chacko2024spectro,sondhi2025jirvis} and transformer elucidation
from IR spectra~\citep{alberts2024irstruct,alberts2024multimodal}.
Models and retrieval tools that consume literature peak lists need
redistributable numeric records with source-identifier attribution and explicit licence pools;
IRexp is built as that substrate.

\noindent
IRexp therefore:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Harvests open-access full text from the PMC Open Access Subset bulk S3
        mirror and peak lists from the Chemotion FT-IR deposit.
  \item Extracts deterministic numeric IR band lists (and co-reported NMR strings
        where present).
  \item Resolves compound names to canonical structures with OPSIN~\citep{lowe2011opsin},
        RDKit~\citep{landrum_rdkit}, and SELFIES~\citep{krenn2020selfies} where possible,
        recording InChIKey~\citep{heller2013inchi} when resolution succeeds.
  \item Releases extracted numbers only: no PDFs, figures, or article full text,
        with source accessions for attribution, aligning with FAIR findability and
        reuse~\citep{wilkinson2016fair}.
\end{enumerate}

Among openly redistributable \emph{text-derived IR band lists}, IRexp's public
release is the commercial file in Section~\ref{sec:datarecords}.
SDBS is a view-only archive of digitised absorbance spectra
($\sim$54,100 FT-IR entries).
That commercial pool is a redistributable collection of text-mined band lists.
The two resources are complementary object types.
This Descriptor contributes the curated dataset, harvest provenance, licence
segregation, and validation artefacts.
Intended reuse includes multimodal pretraining and supervised IR-to-structure
modelling on structure-linked commercial rows, and serving as
the literature substrate for complementary elucidation benchmarks if needed.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

Harvest, extraction, licence-join, and validation code live in the
spectro-agent repository (\url{https://github.com/IlkhamFY/spectro-agent}; see
Section~\ref{sec:codeavail}).
Principal modules are named briefly below; the full script inventory is in
Code Availability.

\subsection{Source corpora}

\paragraph{PMC Open Access Subset.}
Primary harvest uses the NCBI PMC OA bulk distribution on Amazon S3
(\texttt{s3://pmc-oa-opendata}, HTTPS endpoint
\texttt{https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com})~\citep{pmc_oa}.
Harvest window: the bulk S3 IR crawl and \texttt{seen\_papers} /
\texttt{ir\_harvest\_snapshot} artefacts were produced on 2026-06-07 (UTC)
(incremental auto-snapshots that day through $\sim$134,893 raw IR rows).
Chemotion was ingested the same day.
A harvest snapshot records 188,016 distinct PMC identifiers scanned for the
main-text harvest.
A second harvest in October 2026 read the supporting-information (SI) files of
37,724 PMC OA papers.
The combined frozen list holds 220,159 distinct PMC identifiers
(\texttt{data/seen\_papers.txt.gz}).
The released corpus retains records from 20,573 unique \texttt{PMC:*} accessions.
Extraction operates on open-access plain text objects at flat S3 keys
\texttt{PMC<id>.<v>/PMC<id>.<v>.txt}.
Europe PMC full-text XML~\citep{rosonovski2024europepmc} is used for some validation re-fetches.
Released \texttt{source\_doi} values are \texttt{PMC:*} accessions or the Chemotion
deposit DOI; no paywalled publisher DOI appears in the research corpus
(Section~\ref{sec:datarecords}).

\paragraph{Discovery vs \texttt{oa\_comm} / \texttt{oa\_noncomm} packages.}
Identifier discovery used NCBI E-utilities \texttt{esearch} over PMC with an
IR-characterisation query and \texttt{open access[filter]}, then fetched matching
IDs from the flat S3 text layout.
The harvest therefore did not pre-filter by walking the PMC OA Subset's
\texttt{oa\_comm} vs \texttt{oa\_noncomm} vs other package directories.
PMC OA is still not a single licence~\citep{pmc_oa}.
Licence truth for redistribution is applied post-hoc: every unique PMCID of the
main-text harvest (15,416) was joined to Europe PMC \texttt{license} metadata, and
SI rows take the article licence from the PMC OA S3 metadata; each record carries
\texttt{license} / \texttt{license\_pool}.
SI rows from articles without a redistributable licence were not kept.
A second-pass recovery promoted 1,818 previously empty/unknown rows into
commercial, non-commercial, or other pools when Crossref~\citep{kemp2022crossref}
exposed an explicit Creative Commons or ACS AuthorChoice CC URL, or when a fresh
Europe PMC query returned a licence string; Elsevier/Springer TDM-only Crossref
links were ignored.
Commercial-use (CC-BY/CC0) rows form the primary archival
pool~\citep{hrynaszkiewicz2012open}.
NC* rows are deposited as a separate Hub companion under their source licence
and are excluded from that pool.
Empty/unknown rows are not redistributed.
Licence assignment follows article-level terms rather than
S3 package paths (\texttt{data/NOTICE}; \texttt{docs/LICENCE\_REMEDIATION.md}).

\paragraph{Reproducibility of discovery.}
The frozen discovery set for this release is
\texttt{data/seen\_papers.txt.gz} (220,159 PMC identifiers), deposited on the
Hub and Zenodo.
The curated release retains 20,573 of those accessions.
Third parties should treat \texttt{seen\_papers} + the released JSONL as the
reproducible snapshot, not a fresh API crawl.

\paragraph{Chemotion / RADAR4Chem.}
1,888 records come from the Chemotion Repository~\citep{tremouilhac2020chemotionrepo}
FT-IR collection (ELN ecosystem~\citep{tremouilhac2017chemotion}) deposited at
RADAR4Chem (DOI \texttt{10.22000/OGoEQGlsZGElrgst})~\citep{chemotion2024}, licensed
CC-BY-SA-4.0.
Ingest (2026-06-07): download the MD5-verified deposit; for each of 2,116
ATR-IR analyses, flatten the Quill-delta \texttt{content} field to plain text;
parse an author-curated IR band list with the same regex extractor and the
same IR-window and band-count construction filters as the PMC path; resolve the deposit's canonical SMILES with RDKit
$\to$ InChIKey + SELFIES; keep the richest band list per InChIKey; drop the 8
molecules already present in the PMC pool $\to$ $+1{,}888$ new structure-resolved
rows.
All 1,888 Chemotion rows carry a deposit structure.
1,802 are released as structure-linked (\texttt{has\_structure=true}; tier~D
because the rows have no NMR; \texttt{inchikey} equals the record \texttt{id} on
these rows), and 86 salt or invalid deposit structures are withheld (tier~X).
That equality is a convenience for this row type
(Section~\ref{sec:datarecords}).
These are not algorithmic peak-picks from absorbance curves; they are
author-entered experimental band lists from the ELN deposit, denser than typical
paper prose (median 39 bands vs 10 for PMC).
Rows carry \texttt{license=CC-BY-SA-4.0}, \texttt{source=Chemotion}, and
\texttt{source\_doi} equal to the deposit DOI.

\paragraph{Excluded.}
AIST SDBS (view-only; no bulk export)~\citep{sdbs}. NIST Chemistry WebBook gas-phase IR entries are largely historical mid--late 20th century digitizations and remain view-only; they are not in released IRexp rows (join code exists in the repository but contributes 0 records; \texttt{ir\_source} is always \texttt{experimental}).

\subsection{Discovery and fetch (released corpus)}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Discover IR-reporting OA PMCIDs via NCBI \texttt{esearch}
        (\texttt{open access[filter]} + characterisation-format IR query;
        year/month sliced).
  \item Fetch OA full text from PMC-OA S3 plain-text objects
        (\texttt{PMC<id>.<v>.txt}).
  \item Ingest Chemotion deposit author-curated band lists + structures.
  \item Persist harvest provenance in \texttt{data/seen\_papers.txt.gz}
        (this repository) and optional raw rows in
        \texttt{ir\_harvest\_snapshot.jsonl.gz} (134,893 rows including
        intermediate prose fields; not deposited here; the curated research
        corpus is in Section~\ref{sec:datarecords}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Publisher adapters (non-release).}
Non-release publisher-page adapters are not on the IRexp construction path; the
published corpus uses only PMC-OA S3 and Chemotion.

\subsection{Extraction (band lists, not spectra)}

A deterministic regular-expression pipeline (\texttt{spectro\_scraper/extract.py})
segments experimental text per compound and extracts IR/NMR strings
(complementary to general chemical information-extraction toolkits such as
ChemDataExtractor~\citep{swain2016chemdataextractor}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item IR wavenumbers $\to$ \texttt{ir\_bands\_cm-1} (list of floats, cm$^{-1}$);
  \item $^{1}$H and $^{13}$C payloads $\to$ \texttt{h\_nmr} / \texttt{c\_nmr}
        (author strings, when present).
\end{itemize}
Gates reject scan-range artefacts and common prose false positives.
A parser repair handles US thousands separators in IR lists: before
matching, \texttt{\_parse\_ir\_bands} strips grouping commas (for example
PMC6268696 reports $2{,}976$\,cm$^{-1}$, previously stored as 976 and now as
2976).
The commercial file keeps that prior list in
\texttt{ir\_bands\_old\_cm-1}.
After the repair the $\geq$3 band filter was reapplied, removing 26 rows that
then had fewer than three bands, and duplicate integer wavenumbers were removed
within each remaining list (1,432 commercial rows; the rows themselves were kept).
Forty commercial rows are marked \texttt{ir\_refetch\_merged}, recording band
positions gained from a source re-fetch.
No curve digitisation is performed: figures are not traced; JCAMP-DX is not
stored.

\subsection{Structure resolution}

Where an IUPAC or systematic name is available, names are converted with
OPSIN~\citep{lowe2011opsin}, canonicalised with RDKit~\citep{landrum_rdkit}, and
encoded as InChIKey~\citep{heller2013inchi} and SELFIES~\citep{krenn2020selfies}.
An optional PubChem~\citep{kim2023pubchem} name fallback (\texttt{USE\_PUBCHEM})
handles trivial names.
Name-to-structure failures are expected for trade names, mixtures, and
non-IUPAC prose; unresolved rows keep \texttt{has\_structure=false} and null
structure fields.
Structure coverage of the research corpus is 42.4\% (101,624 / 239,735);
every released structure carries an evidence tier (next subsection).
The multi-licence structure-linked split is \texttt{irexp\_resolved}
(research corpus).
The public structure-linked commercial subset is
\texttt{data/irexp\_resolved\_commercial.jsonl.gz}.

\subsection{IR attachment, supporting information, and structure tiers}\label{sec:v02}

\paragraph{IR attachment.}
The extractor assigns IR blocks within each compound's own section of the
experimental text, bounded by compound headings and paragraph breaks, instead of
attaching the nearest IR block to an NMR record.
A record without an IR block of its own gets no IR, and IR-only compounds become
their own records.
The main-text part of the research corpus is 116,320 PMC rows.

\paragraph{Supporting-information expansion.}
The same parser read the SI files of PMC OA papers and added 121,527 band lists
from 5,688 papers (78,053 commercial and 43,474 non-commercial).
The construction filters are the same as for the main text: at least three
bands, 400--4000\,cm$^{-1}$, within-list integer-band deduplication, and removal of
rows already present in the main-text corpus (1,836).
On SI rows \texttt{ir\_source} is \texttt{experimental\_si}, the author IR string
is kept in \texttt{ir\_raw}, and the compound label and name are kept in
\texttt{si\_label} and \texttt{si\_name}.

\paragraph{Structure tiers.}
Structures come from OPSIN on compound names in the record's own block, a label
to name map within the same paper, PubChem name lookup, and optical chemical
structure recognition (OCSR) of the compound drawings in the SI.
Every released structure carries \texttt{structure\_tier}
(Table~\ref{tab:tiers}).
Tier~A needs a formula or HRMS match in the compound's own block and an
independent second method that agrees on the InChIKey-14 (PubChem on the name,
or OCSR on the drawing).
OPSIN on the same name string is not counted as independent, and OCSR alone is
never tier~A.
A method is used for a tier only when it reaches at least 97\% precision on
held-out rows.
Tier~X rows keep their band lists, but their structure is withheld
(\texttt{has\_structure=false}).
Formula mismatches that are likely typing errors in the source are flagged
(\texttt{formula\_conflict}) rather than excluded.

\subsection{Licence handling}

Licence-join, Crossref recovery, and pool-split tooling stamps every row and
materialises pool files (Section~\ref{sec:codeavail}).
Research-corpus pool stamps sum to 239,735 (Figure~\ref{fig:distribution}b);
public file counts are in Table~\ref{tab:files}.
Policy is in \texttt{docs/LICENCE\_REMEDIATION.md}.

\subsection{Quality tooling}

IR-range and band-count gates are construction filters
(at least three bands; wavenumbers in 400--4000\,cm$^{-1}$;
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}).
On the commercial file the band-count gate was reapplied after
the thousands-separator repair, and duplicate integer bands were removed
within each list.
Every released wavenumber lies in that window (Technical Validation).
Formula--NMR physics checks in \texttt{spectro\_scraper.quality}
($^{1}$H integral sum $\leq$ formula hydrogen count $+2$; $^{13}$C peak count
$\leq$ carbon count) are diagnostic, post-hoc quarantine flags.
Flagged rows remain in the release files; Technical Validation reports the
full-corpus quarantine on \texttt{irexp\_resolved}.

\section{Data Records}\label{sec:datarecords}

\paragraph{Release scope.}
The multi-licence research corpus holds 239,735 records, of which 101,624 are
structure-linked and 54,429 are IR~$+$~$^{1}$H~$+$~$^{13}$C~$+$~structure
quadruples.
That total is construction and licence-pool accounting.
It is not deposited as a Hub file.
The public dataset is the commercial pool only:
\texttt{data/irexp\_commercial.jsonl.gz} (163,931 CC-BY/CC0 records) on Hugging Face
(\texttt{ilkhamfy/IRexp}; revision in the Access subsection)~\citep{irexp_hf}
and archived on Zenodo~\citep{irexp_zenodo_v02}, packaged under
CC-BY-4.0, with per-record source licences still stamped.
The same Hub revision includes \texttt{data/irexp\_resolved\_commercial.jsonl.gz}
(67,235; the commercial rows with a released structure, tiers A--D) and
\texttt{data/train\_no\_bench\_commercial.jsonl.gz} (67,093),
both subsets of the commercial pool.
Two further files on that revision are licence-bound companions and do not
enter the commercial file:
\texttt{data/irexp\_sharealike.jsonl.gz} (1,897; Chemotion plus rare PMC
ShareAlike; source licence CC-BY-SA-4.0) and
\texttt{data/irexp\_non\_commercial.jsonl.gz} (65,007; NC*).
The public downloadable total of the commercial, ShareAlike, and
non-commercial files is 230,835 (163,931 $+$ 65,007 $+$ 1,897).
\texttt{data/irexp\_other.jsonl.gz} (5; CC-BY-ND) is on the same Hub revision
and is not in the commercial file ($n=163{,}931$).
Empty/unknown (8,895) is not a Hub file.
8,900 research-corpus rows lie outside the commercial, ShareAlike, and
non-commercial files (8,895 empty/unknown $+$ 5 CC-BY-ND).

\subsection{Object definition}

Each IRexp record is a JSON object; see Table~\ref{tab:fields}.
The \texttt{id} field is an opaque stable key (a mix of hashes and
InChIKey-shaped strings), not a guaranteed molecule join key.
InChIKey is stored in \texttt{inchikey} and is not unique across the corpus:
101,624 structure-linked records comprise 97,374 InChIKeys (about 4.3 thousand
extra rows repeat a molecule).
Non-commercial and ShareAlike companions use the core columns.
The commercial file adds QC columns: \texttt{ir\_bands\_old\_cm-1},
\texttt{ir\_shared\_in\_paper}, \texttt{ir\_table\_flatten\_suspect},
\texttt{ir\_refetch\_confirmed}, and \texttt{ir\_refetch\_merged}
(flag-only where boolean; rows not dropped).
SI rows carry the same QC columns; there \texttt{ir\_bands\_old\_cm-1} is the list
before within-list integer deduplication and \texttt{ir\_table\_flatten\_suspect}
is null (not computed).
\texttt{source\_doi} is a source identifier, not always a DOI.
A representative structure-linked record from the commercial file
is shown in Listing~\ref{lst:example}: nine experimental IR bands
(cm$^{-1}$) mined from PMC13029360, with a resolved SMILES/InChIKey and
co-reported $^{1}$H/$^{13}$C NMR strings on the row.

\section{Technical Validation}\label{sec:techval}

Machine-readable package: \texttt{data/qc\_structure\_nmr.json} in this
repository (audit artefacts under \texttt{data/audit/} in the code repository).
Numbers below are seed-fixed descriptive checks; Wilson 95\% intervals are
reported where a check computed them.
PRE is the earlier multi-licence research release (121,233 records; PMC pool
119,345; 15,416 PMCIDs; chemist strata include Chemotion and other licences).
POST repeats the same scripts, seeds, and sample sizes on the previous commercial
file (Hub revision \texttt{4312254}, $n=88{,}519$), which carries
the thousands-separator repair.
The pools differ, so each pair is a side-by-side description, not a paired
difference.
Figure~\ref{fig:distribution}f shows the PRE sample distributions.

\subsection{Transcription fidelity (PMC)}

On a seed-fixed random sample of 60 PMC-sourced records from the research
release, each article was re-fetched from Europe PMC and every recorded
wavenumber was checked against the source text ($\pm$1\,cm$^{-1}$ integer match).
Result: 560/560 bands and 60/60 records confirmed.
The enlarged sample is $n=200$ (same script and seed).
PRE: 2,250/2,261 bands (0.9951) and 196/200 records fully confirmed.
The four incomplete records (PMCIDs 5713685, 4189708, 12202350, 6259152) missed
11 bands total on re-fetch match.
POST ($n=88{,}519$): 2,117/2,119 bands
(0.9991) and 199/200 records fully confirmed.
One incomplete record (PMC6835751) missed two bands.
This bounds automated transcription error (mis-parsed or unit-mangled values).

\subsection{Extraction-recall proxy (PMC)}

As a proxy: re-fetch PMC-OA S3 plain text
for 120 distinct source PMCIDs; re-run extraction; compare band-sets to released
rows ($\pm$1\,cm$^{-1}$).
PRE: 7,981/8,059 released bands confirmed in re-extract (0.9903;
Wilson 95\% CI $[0.9879, 0.9922]$);
845/858 released IR lists recovered (list-level recall proxy 0.9848;
Wilson 95\% CI $[0.9743, 0.9911]$);
115/120 papers recovered every released list (0.9583;
Wilson 95\% CI $[0.9062, 0.9821]$).
On that PRE re-extract, 811 of 871 IR lists matched a released list (0.9311);
18/120 papers yielded \emph{extra} re-extracted lists relative to the curated
release (parser over-fire vs post-harvest dedup/curation, not missed released
compounds).
POST ($n=88{,}519$): list-level recall proxy 0.9739 (1,043/1,071);
released bands in the re-extract 0.9781 (12,132/12,404);
118/120 papers recovered every released list (0.9833).
POST overlap is lower because the re-extract does not apply the
separator repair.
Residual misses are concentrated in two PMCIDs (segmenter/\texttt{\_IR\_RE}
gaps, not thousands separators).

\section{Usage Notes}\label{sec:usage}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Band lists $\neq$ spectra.} Do not evaluate models trained on IRexp as
        if they had seen full absorbance curves.
  \item \textbf{Licence filter.} The public dataset is the commercial
        file only (\texttt{data/irexp\_commercial.jsonl.gz}; CC-BY-4.0 packaging;
        per-record CC-BY/CC0 stamps; Section~\ref{sec:datarecords}).
        ShareAlike (1,897; \texttt{data/irexp\_sharealike.jsonl.gz}; source
        licence CC-BY-SA-4.0) and non-commercial (65,007;
        \texttt{data/irexp\_non\_commercial.jsonl.gz}; NC*) are separate Hub
        companions on the same revision.
        They remain bound by their source licences and are not in the
        commercial file.
        Do not relicence Chemotion or other ShareAlike rows as CC-BY.
        ND (5; CC-BY-ND; \texttt{data/irexp\_other.jsonl.gz}) is a Hub companion
        not in the commercial file.
        Empty/unknown rows and the multi-licence total
        \texttt{irexp.jsonl.gz} are not Hub files.
        Attribute via \texttt{source\_doi} / \texttt{pmcid}.
        \texttt{source\_doi} may hold a PMC accession (\texttt{PMC:\ldots}) rather
        than a DOI.
  \item \textbf{Separate pools by density and licence.} PMC (sparse, author-transcribed)
        vs Chemotion (denser, author-curated ELN band lists).
        Higher median band count does not imply more complete vibrational
        assignment; Chemotion lists are author-entered, not peak-picked curves.
  \item \textbf{Structure--NMR quarantine.} Drop IDs in
        \texttt{data/structure\_quarantine.jsonl.gz} by default before
        supervised structure or NMR training
        (0.3\% of structure-linked rows, plus tier-X ids whose structures are
        already withheld) unless a noisier training set is intentional.
        The commercial part of that list is on the Zenodo deposit
        (\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23463965}).
  \item \textbf{Structure tiers.} Use \texttt{structure\_tier} A--B when a
        formula-verified structure is required; tiers C--D add coverage at
        lower evidence.
  \item \textbf{Training without benchmark leakage.} If holding out structures used
        in a complementary elucidation benchmark, withhold those InChIKeys.
        \texttt{train\_no\_bench.jsonl.gz} is the multi-licence research split
        (rebuild with \texttt{scripts/build\_train\_no\_bench.py}).
        Commercial training uses
        \texttt{data/train\_no\_bench\_commercial.jsonl.gz} on the Hub revision
        (Section~\ref{sec:dataavail}).
        Both files withhold the 248 InChIKey-14 keys of the locked benchmark
        rounds (locked 194 and electrolyte).
        Expansion-round keys are withheld from the review copy and are not
        filtered, so fine-tuned models should be scored on the locked 194.
  \item \textbf{Structure coverage.} Supervised structure tasks need rows with
        SMILES; 57.6\% of the research corpus lack a released structure.
        Use \texttt{data/irexp\_resolved\_commercial.jsonl.gz}
        (Section~\ref{sec:datarecords}).
  \item \textbf{FAIR reuse example.} For a redistributable training set, load the
        commercial file; drop IDs on the research-corpus quarantine list when
        that list is applied; keep \texttt{source\_doi} /
        \texttt{pmcid} for attribution; prefer structure-linked commercial rows
        when SMILES is required.
        Chemotion and other ShareAlike rows are in the public ShareAlike
        companion and may be remixed only under CC-BY-SA-4.0.
  \item \textbf{Attribution.} Cite this Data Descriptor and each record's
        \texttt{source\_doi} (PMC accession or DOI).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Object.} IRexp is a band-list corpus, not an absorbance-spectrum
        library; co-reported NMR strings are retained where present but are not
        a stand-alone NMR resource.
  \item \textbf{Technical Validation depth.} Automated transcription ($n=200$),
        harvest-path recall proxies ($n=120$ papers), and a stratified consistency
        audit ($n=280$) are machine checks, reported on the earlier research
        release and the previous commercial file;
        the v0.2 additions are covered by a 150-record human audit.
        A stratified expert human band-list audit scored 161 records and was
        not repeated after the separator repair.
        No full-corpus human molecular-skeleton audit has been completed for IRexp.
  \item \textbf{Metadata sparsity.} Intensities, solvents, and instrument modes are
        generally absent; the IR window check is necessary but narrow.
  \item \textbf{Structure coverage and name resolution.} Only 42.4\% of records are
        structure-linked and 23.4\% are formula-verified (tiers A--B);
        OPSIN/PubChem/OCSR failures leave many IR lists without SMILES.
  \item \textbf{Licence mix.} The research corpus is multi-licence.
        The public dataset is the commercial pool
        (Section~\ref{sec:datarecords}).
        ShareAlike and NC* companions are on the same Hub repository under
        their source licences and are excluded from that pool.
        The CC-BY-ND companion (5) is excluded from that pool.
        Empty/unknown rows are not Hub files.
\end{itemize}
